Privacy Policy

Effective EFFECTIVE_DATE

Term is an iPhone app made by Nathanael Griffin, an individual developer, who is responsible for the data described here. Questions go to SUPPORT_EMAIL.

In short

  • There is no account and no sign-in.
  • There are no ads, no tracking, and no third-party analytics or crash-reporting code in the app.
  • Your hosts, keys, passwords, terminal content and files stay on your iPhone and go only to the computers you connect to.
  • If you turn on dictation with your own OpenRouter API key, your recordings and some terminal text go to OpenRouter under your account.
  • The developer runs one small service, used for push notifications and a daily usage count. Neither carries terminal content, and the count can be turned off.

Term is open source. You can check all of this in the code at REPO_URL.

What stays on your iPhone

Term keeps these on your iPhone:

  • Your hosts: their names, addresses, user names and settings, and the host keys Term pinned for them.
  • SSH keys made or imported in the app, and host passwords, in the iOS Keychain.
  • The Mac password for Screen Sharing, if you enter one, in the Keychain behind Face ID.
  • Your OpenRouter API key, if you add one, in the Keychain.
  • Terminal content and history, files you open, and your settings.
  • A diagnostic log of the app's own decisions (connections, gestures, menu actions) used to fix bugs. It never records what you type or what the terminal shows. It stays in the app's storage on your iPhone and is not sent anywhere.

Term sends your sessions, keystrokes and files only to the computers you add, directly from your iPhone over SSH, mosh and SFTP. The developer runs no server that sees them and has no access to your computers.

Screen

Term Screen Host is a small app you install on your Mac. It sends the Mac's screen and clipboard only to your iPhone, through your own SSH connection to the Mac. It opens nothing new on the network.

Dictation

Dictation is optional and off until you add your own OpenRouter API key in Settings › Voice. When you dictate, Term sends these directly from your iPhone to OpenRouter, under your OpenRouter account:

  • the audio you recorded;
  • a context prompt that helps the transcription: the last lines visible in that terminal, the text you last dictated into it, and your list of words to get right.

If Clean Up Dictation is on (Settings › Voice), Term also sends the transcript and your list of words to get right to a small text model, also through OpenRouter.

This means some terminal text leaves your iPhone when you dictate. OpenRouter's handling of it is covered by OpenRouter's privacy policy. The developer never receives any of it. Without a key, nothing goes to OpenRouter. Remove the key in Settings › Voice to stop.

Push notifications

If you allow notifications, the host kit on your computer can tell you when a session needs you. It sends a relay service, run by the developer on Cloudflare Workers, two things: your iPhone's push token (an address Apple issues so notifications reach this app on this iPhone) and a notice that names the session. The relay forwards them to Apple's push notification service, which delivers the notification.

The notice carries the tmux session's name, so name sessions with that in mind. It carries no terminal content.

Daily count

Once a day, Term sends the same service one request so the developer can count how many people use the app and which parts. It contains:

  • a random install id, made on your iPhone and replaced every month;
  • four yes-or-no flags: whether a host connected, whether Screen was opened, whether you answered an agent's question, and whether you have Pro.

It contains no hostnames, no terminal content and nothing that names you. The service does not store IP addresses.

To turn it off, use the switch in Settings › COUNTER_SWITCH. Nothing is sent while it is off.

Purchases

Purchases and subscriptions go through Apple. The developer sees only Apple's aggregate sales and subscription reports, never your name, Apple Account or payment details.

How long data is kept

Data on your iPhone stays until you delete it. Remove hosts and keys in Settings › Hosts and Settings › Keys, or delete the app.

The relay service keeps push tokens, notices and daily counts for RETENTION_DAYS days, then deletes them.

Your questions and requests

Nothing the service stores identifies you: the install id is random and changes every month, and there is no account. So the developer cannot look up or hand over data about a particular person. To stop the daily count, turn it off in Settings. To stop notifications, turn them off for Term in the iPhone's Settings. For any question, write to SUPPORT_EMAIL.

Children

Term is not directed at children under 13, and the developer does not knowingly collect data from them.
